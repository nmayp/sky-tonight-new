# What "you will see" means in this project

The product answers: for a place and a time, what can a person looking up expect to see? Labels for checking the
model must follow one written rule, so two people labelling the same picture agree.

## Sky state (one label per picture, judged for the whole visible sky)

| label | what you see | rough cloud cover |
|---|---|---|
| **clear** | stars across the whole sky; no cloud or only a few small wisps | 0 to 2 eighths |
| **partly** | clear gaps and cloud or haze both present; or the whole sky veiled so only the brighter stars show | 3 to 5 eighths, or thin haze everywhere |
| **cloudy** | most of the sky covered, or no stars except the very brightest | 6 to 8 eighths |

The product target of the model ("clear") is cloud of at most 2 eighths, the same definition used by airport
SYNOP reports, so labels, observations and the model share one meaning.

## Things that change what a person sees but are NOT cloud (note them, do not fold them into the label)

- **Moon:** a bright Moon washes out faint stars and lights clouds from below (a moonlit overcast looks bright).
- **Twilight:** only judge pictures when the Sun is more than 12 degrees below the horizon.
- **Fog, dust, smoke:** count as haze.
- **Local light pollution** is a separate measure (night-lights index, later sky-brightness meters).
- **Camera effects:** long exposures make a night camera show more stars than an eye would; an ordinary webcam
  at night may show only black. Say which camera a label came from.

## Second labeller

At least 30 pictures are labelled blind by a second person (no model output, no first label shown). Report the
share of agreement and the disagreements; labels that two people disagree on are not used to score the model.
